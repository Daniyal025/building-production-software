# S6 rubric (held-out, independent and blind)

Written by a separate agent that never saw the skills, **after** the v2.1 fixes were made. Neither the skills nor the fixes were shaped against it.

**Task:** "We want an internal Q&A assistant over our ~2,000 internal PDFs and wiki pages (policies, runbooks, product docs) for about 50 employees. Some documents are restricted to certain teams. Plan ingestion, retrieval, answering, evaluation and rollout. Keep it to ~20 bullets."

1. Permissions are enforced at retrieval time: each chunk carries ACL or team metadata copied from its source, the search query filters on the asking user's identity or groups before ranking (not by filtering the LLM's output afterwards), and the answer names how the user's groups are resolved (SSO/IdP groups).
2. Permission changes reach the index: the plan says how ACL changes, revoked access and deleted or moved documents get into the index (re-sync of permissions, removing tombstoned docs) and gives a staleness bound, so a restricted or deleted doc stops showing up within a stated time.
3. Ingestion handles messy sources concretely: PDF text extraction with OCR as a fallback for scanned pages, chunking that respects document structure (headings, tables, wiki sections), and metadata (title, URL, owner, last-updated) is kept for citations.
4. Incremental refresh and parse failures are covered: changed documents are detected by hash or modified time, and docs that fail to parse are logged or listed so someone can review them. They are not silently dropped, and the plan does not re-embed the whole corpus on a schedule without a reason.
5. Retrieval is a reasonable choice for the scale: hybrid search (BM25 + embeddings), optionally with a reranker, justified by exact-term queries such as runbook names, error codes and policy IDs, with a top-k or context budget stated.
6. Answers are grounded and cited: the model answers only from the retrieved passages, and every claim links to a source doc or section the user can click through to and has access to.
7. Abstention and failure behaviour is defined: when retrieval is weak or empty, or sources conflict, the assistant says "I don't know" or points to the doc owner instead of guessing, and when the LLM or search is down it fails clearly (no partial or uncited answer).
8. Restricted content cannot leak through side channels: prompt-injection text inside documents is treated as data, nothing restricted leaks through caching, logs, conversation history or "related docs" suggestions, and the plan states what is logged and who can read the logs.
9. Evaluation has a concrete gold set before launch: roughly 50–150 real questions with expected source docs or answers, written with the doc owners, which include questions that must be refused for a given user's role. The set measures retrieval (recall@k) apart from answer quality (faithfulness, correctness) and has a pass bar.
10. Permission tests are explicit and automated: test users from different teams ask the same questions, and the check asserts that zero restricted chunks are retrieved or cited. The check runs again on every index or pipeline change, as a release gate.
11. Rollout is staged with a feedback loop: a pilot with a small group, which can include the teams that own restricted docs, then org-wide. Answers get a thumbs up/down or "report wrong answer" action that feeds the gold set, and someone is named to own content gaps and stale docs.
12. Proportionate to ~2,000 docs and 50 users: uses a managed or single-node vector store (e.g. pgvector or the platform's built-in search), with no fine-tuning, no custom embedding training, no multi-agent orchestration and no Kubernetes-scale infrastructure. Gives a rough cost and latency estimate, stays near the ~20-bullet limit, and does not pad the plan with extra scope.
